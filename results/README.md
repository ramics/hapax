# Experiment archive

One line per experiment: what it measured, what it settled, and where to read it. The spec
that these results justify is [`../SPEC.md`](../SPEC.md).

**Everything here is kept, including what was killed and what was withdrawn.** The negatives
are load-bearing — they are why the spec's target is a measurement rather than a call — so
nothing is deleted for tidiness. Where a result has been superseded or retracted, the row
says so and the files stay put.

Conventions: every script takes `--seed` and writes a run manifest (seed, git SHA,
parameters, timings) beside its output; headline results run across at least five seeds with
spread reported. Scripts live in [`../experiments/`](../experiments/) under the same name as
their results directory.

## The current direction — subtract the known, measure the residue

| directory | what it measured | status |
|---|---|---|
| [`readlevel_features/`](readlevel_features/REPORT.md) | Ceilings on single-read features — ORF-likeness, k-mer composition, codon usage, GC. None has novelty polarity, because a wastewater background is coding too. Includes the prior-art review and the Viromancer/Lyra-Virus review. | live; the reason no axis is thresholded |
| [`residue_structure/`](residue_structure/REPORT.md) | SPEC.md §4.2's gate experiment: is the residue structured or diffuse in the joint space? **No clusters beyond its own covariance (k=1, 5/5 seeds), the joint space beats the best single axis by 0.013 AUC, but a planted genome's reads find each other by neighbourhood down to about 1 in 100,000 and not below.** Adds exact whole-library 31-mer coverage shape as an axis set. | live; the gate is answered |
| [`residue_census/`](residue_census/REPORT.md) | What the residue is MADE OF, as three measurements with their controls in the same pass. **Not artefact**: RNA-seq library artefacts — fold-back hairpins, same-strand mates, short inserts, poly(A/T), vector, PhiX — are under 1% of the residue, and the two fields that do point somewhere (UniVec, poly(A/T)) point at the *identified* pile. **Not hidden abundant genomes**: the 1,372,021 pairs (15.71%) with neither mate matching SILVA or any own contig at >=40 bases assemble into 3,695 contigs totalling 1.31 Mb, median 331 nt, 7 at >=1 kb, recruiting 3.8% of themselves back. **Thin**: exact whole-library 31-mer counts put that pile at **median depth 1**, 91.5% below 5 occurrences and 32.5% fully unwitnessed, against 86.6 for identified reads and ~3,000 for ribosomal — below the ~1-in-100,000 floor where neighbourhood grouping dies, so anything needing two observations of a molecule has nothing to work with. Also ships the validated whole-library label table for all 8,731,224 pairs. | live, 5 seeds; the contig half is [`residue_census/CONTIGS.md`](residue_census/CONTIGS.md) |
| [`linkage_census/`](linkage_census/REPORT.md) | What the residue sits NEXT TO, from the adjacency a paired library gives free: label the other mates of the library pairs covering the same sequence. **A residue read's neighbourhood is 92% sequence that is itself unidentified; an identified read's is 94% nameable** (5 seeds). At most 3.3% of the residue is unidentified for annotation reasons, and its permuted-label null is 2.9% of that. 30% of residue pairs have no witnesses at all and are reported apart, never folded into the unknown. | live; the residue is not an annotation artefact |
| [`residue_contig_annotation/`](residue_contig_annotation/REPORT.md) | What the residue contigs ARE, by profile rather than index: Prodigal coding density, Pfam-A and Rfam at their own gathering thresholds, against three length-matched controls drawn from the sample's own contigs and a dinucleotide-preserving shuffle. **The two residue piles answer in opposite directions.** The 200 highest-mass unidentifiable contigs are LESS coding than length-matched unidentifiable ones (0.604 against 0.818) and lead every pile on Rfam (3.0%), so the deepest unnameable sequence is non-coding and not a hidden virus. Nor is it ribosomal at the cascade's operating point: this report first read it that way on 6 Rfam hits against 2, and `results/residue_census/top_contig_rrna_s*.json` killed that directly - these 200 read a median 0 matching bases against SILVA with none above the split's 100-base line, where the known-positive rRNA arm reads 464 and clears it on every contig. That instrument cannot see divergent rRNA, so the claim is scoped to the threshold. The 2,832 genuinely new contigs from unplaced pairs run the other way: 23.1% carry a Pfam family and **11.7% an RdRp against 0.70% for the length-matched unidentifiable control**, dominated by Leviviridae replicase. The ribosomal covariance models fire on 1.80% of new contigs against a 0.54-0.62% floor on real length-matched non-ribosomal sequence and 0.00% on the shuffle - but the already-seen re-derived arm reads 2.51%, higher still, so the excess tracks *assembled from shallow unplaced pairs* rather than *new*, and is not an annotation gap. | live, 5 seeds; the new-contig input is a superset of the cascade residue (no read-level Kraken2 or DIAMOND behind its labels), which inflates the protein numbers and cannot deflate them |
| [`block_position_bias/`](block_position_bias/REPORT.md) | Whether a contiguous block of the FASTQ is a random sample of the library, since every percentage in this repo comes from one and the per-read sampling is nested inside it. Measured exactly over all 8,731,224 labelled pairs, with uniform random samples as the control (0.92-1.10 on the same statistic). **Location is fine and dispersion is not.** The five cascade blocks sit within 0.14 points of the whole library on every class and within 0.03 on both residue classes, and block 1 - the first tile, the prime suspect - is inside +-1.1 z. But the blocks differ from each other by more than sampling noise: **overdispersion 1.57x on identified and 1.43x on rRNA**, with adjacent blocks correlated at +0.28 and +0.24, so those Wilson intervals are that much too narrow. **The residue classes are nearly clean** (1.13-1.15x, autocorrelation within noise of zero), so the structure is in the abundant material and not in what this tool is about. | live, 5 seeds; the factors are now applied as a design effect in `hapax.py` and shown beside the Wilson interval on the web view |
| [`mate_friends/`](mate_friends/REPORT.md) | Mate linkage as the label-free ground truth for "same molecule": each MATE a point, is a read's own partner among its 10 nearest neighbours? **23× chance with no shared 31-mer (5 seeds, 0.58% against 0.025%), null on chance.** As the space grows eightfold the multiplier rises ~1.5× while the level falls as 1/n, so linkage is a real multiplier on the geometry and not a locator — it does not move the 1-in-100,000 wall. The mate direction asymmetry is entirely length, and matching the lengths nearly doubles recovery. | live; the grouping step's calibration |
| [`residue_census/CONTIGS.md`](residue_census/CONTIGS.md) | The depth-6 half — what contig-level work can reach. **77% of the residue-only contigs are sequence the assembly does not hold and 18% re-derive it** (control: an own-unidentified contig against the rest of the same assembly reads 0.1-0.4% at the same threshold), and that split gave the annotation its third control arm and kept 650 contigs out of their own control pool. **The residue is LESS concentrated than known material** — 762 contigs for half its mass against 112 for identified reads, Gini 0.729 against 0.922 — so there is no small set that is most of it; but **42.6% of its contig mass sits on contigs >=500 nt and 2x**, an order of magnitude more reachable than the 3.87% of *contigs* clearing that bar suggests. **Depth and recognisability are anti-correlated**: the 200 highest-mass unidentifiable contigs are less coding than length-matched ones (0.604 against 0.818) while the new residue contigs carry 11.69% RdRp against 0.70%. The ribosomal explanation of that head is **withdrawn** — 0 of 200 reach SILVA's 100-base threshold where true rRNA contigs all do — and the `asm20` sensitivity arm that would have scoped it further **failed as an instrument** and is kept as that. | live, 5 seeds; reads with [`../residue_contig_annotation/`](residue_contig_annotation/REPORT.md) |

## The three-tier cascade, and the big-index measurements

The narrative for every row in this section is one document,
[`subtract_known/REPORT.md`](subtract_known/REPORT.md); the other directories are its
outputs and are listed separately because each answers a question of its own.

| directory | what it measured | status |
|---|---|---|
| [`subtract_known/`](subtract_known/REPORT.md) | The cascade and the index-size axis. The union of everything identifiable removes **77.10%** of the library (5 seeds); the residue is 22.9%, about 8.4 points of it assembling into unidentifiable contigs and 11.1 points matching no contig at all. **The sample's own data is the most efficient index by orders of magnitude** — 15 MB of its own identified contigs removes 30.33 points on top of 32 GB of Kraken2, and 1.4 MB of its own rRNA contigs beats all 272 MB of SILVA. Thresholds dominate size: one Kraken2 database removes 42.8 / 21.7 / 3.5% of the same reads at confidence 0 / 0.1 / 0.2. | live; the spec's §2 rests on this |
| [`subtract_recut/`](subtract_recut/) | The re-cut per-tier table the cascade is read from, and the residue and subtracted FASTAs every later pass uses as input. | live |
| [`nucleotide_ladder/`](nucleotide_ladder/), [`residue_protladder/`](residue_protladder/) | Removal against index size, nucleotide and protein. **Bigger databases buy hits, not identifications**: across four cumulative protein indexes the residue's "any hit at all" goes 77.7 → 92.4 → 97.6 → 98.9%, flat only against a 100% ceiling, while at bitscore ≥50 it goes 0.00 → 0.00 → 0.36 → 1.15% and is still tripling. 5 seeds. | live; corrects an earlier reading of the same curve |
| [`spike_holdout/`](spike_holdout/) | The only positive control: reads from planted genomes surviving tier 1 at 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 30% divergence — 0.00 / 0.68 / 12.6 / 81.6 / 99.6%. Nucleotide tiers die first and protein is last standing (at 20% divergence nucleotide removes nothing and protein still removes 18%), which is why the spec keeps a protein tier. **The flaw is now resolved by reanalysis rather than a re-run** — the run already measured each genome separately and only the pooled table hid it. The sample's own assembly removes 100% and 96.4% of the two resident genomes at 0% divergence and **0.0% of the clean holdout at every rung**, because a virus absent from the sample cannot be in the sample's assembly. So the 20.8% at 10% divergence that had been read as the sample's own data catching novel viruses is **entirely an artefact of spiking resident material**. The headline survival is unaffected (holdout 0.0 / 1.0 / 12.5 / 79.8 / 99.6, inside the pooled seed ranges at every rung), because the own-assembly tier was redundant with the public indexes for those genomes — and for a genuinely novel virus the protein tier does essentially all the work above 5% (91.3 / 84.8 / 20.0 on the holdout). | live, 5 seeds; `TABLES.md` now carries a per-genome tier table and keeps the pooled one marked inflated |
| [`spike_abundance/`](spike_abundance/) | Assembly and classification against abundance: spike breadth 0% at 0.5× coverage, 97.6% at 10×, and geNomad calls nothing below ~2×. Both fail in the same place. | live |
| [`blast_check/`](blast_check/TABLES.md) | Ground truth from the real public databases rather than our own copies: 60 sequences per class per seed against core_nt. Nearly everything hits something, so the column to read is the strong hit - 95% identity over 80% of the query - which is 89.3% for rRNA, 94.7% for public-nucleotide, 26.7% for reads identified only by the sample's own assembly, 8.7% and 5.7% for the two residue classes and 5.2% for tier-2 contigs (5 seeds, ranges in the table). | live, **5 seeds on one script hash**; the two seeds previously excluded for scoring lost service batches as misses have been re-run, and the figures moved by under two points |
| [`blast_check_x/`](blast_check_x/) | The same ground truth translated: `blastx` against nr, for requirement 1.iii where a nucleotide index is already dead. | in flight |
| [`bigindex/`](bigindex/) | The first measurement of the residue against a **full, pinned, citable public nucleotide database** — our own staged copy of NCBI core_nt (318.8 GB, prefix `2026-10-22-01-05-01`) searched in-region on EC2. **Read the E-value fields, not the hit rate**: 86.8% of residue reads get *some* alignment, but the median one has E = 0.24 and is a chance match. At E < 1e-5, **14.6% have a relative and it aligns over a median 96% of the read at 90.1% identity** — a divergent strain, not novel material — while 7.8% clear the cascade's operating point at 97.7% over 105 of 107 bases. So 318.8 GB buys about 8 points where 15 MB of the sample's own contigs bought 30. | live for the pilot; **the unfiltered reading of this table is withdrawn** in `subtract_known/REPORT.md` §3.4 |
| [`diamond_scaling/`](diamond_scaling/) | What a search costs as a function of database size, and the instrument that exists to stop a fixed cost being quoted as a rate. It can only reach two database sizes, and the linear and superlinear laws it fits disagree by a factor of fifteen at the far end — which is why the big-index run measures its own scan before sizing anything. | live; read it before pricing a search |
| [`partial_alignment/`](partial_alignment/), [`partial_alignment_contigs/`](partial_alignment_contigs/) | Requirement 1.ii — what is happening when part of a sequence matches well and the rest does not. **A negative at read level and a positive at contig level**: once a 107 nt read matches ~74% of itself the leftover is ~10 aa, under any search's floor, so the test returns nothing for 99.6% of reads, and partial matching is not enriched in the residue (23.8% against 19.7%). On contigs the separator is real but continuous — unidentifiable contigs align over 31.5% of themselves at 35.0% identity, identified ones 56.7% at 40.6% — and the chimaera call fires on 0.04% of unidentifiable against 0.15% of identified contigs, the wrong way round and indistinguishable from its own false positives. | live; **this step belongs after assembly, not on reads** |
| [`residue_fields/`](residue_fields/) | The per-read field table with a shuffled null and a known-positive beside every field. Thirteen axes, about 10.5 of them effectively independent, and **none of them discriminating** — the largest standardised difference between residue and identified reads is 0.40 once abundance is controlled. Dimensionality without discrimination. | live; the calibration the spec's report is built from |
| [`shape_axis_ladder/`](shape_axis_ladder/) | The coverage-shape axes from exact whole-library 31-mer counts, each at 0.76–0.78 AUC. | live |
| [`cov_step_join/`](cov_step_join/), [`cov_step_conservation/`](cov_step_conservation/) | Whether a within-read depth step marks a junction. **It does not** (+0.011 against contig depth ratios, CI −0.054 to +0.075); it marks sequence shared between molecules (+0.577 with the number of distinct contigs a read aligns to, holding inside every coverage quartile, and not ribosomal conservation). So the whole coverage-shape family has **familiarity polarity**: 0.78 AUC that means *already seen*, not *new*. | live; a prediction made and killed the same day |
| [`quality_profile/`](quality_profile/) | The library's error rate measured **reference-free**, from the 26–27% of pairs that overlap their own mate: 0.00068 along the body and 0.0019 at the final base, against 0.00206 implied by the Q strings. So [`berlin_quality/`](berlin_quality/)'s figures are Q-derived upper bounds, conservative by about 2.6×, and the quality ramp along the read is real but weak. | live; supersedes the error rates in `berlin_quality/` |

## A second library, and a second nucleic acid

| directory | what it measured | status |
|---|---|---|
| [`dna_rna_pair/`](dna_rna_pair/REPORT.md) | DNA and RNA extracted from the same water on the same morning, plus our own 2021 library through the same code as the control. **The DNA member is a virome, not a metagenome** (0.01% rRNA against 38-67%, top taxon *Darmviridae*, viral proteins identifying 7.5x what cellular ones do) and **99.1% of it is unidentified** at the cascade's operating point, where 0.24 GB of viral protein beats a 16 GB nucleotide index by 8.5x. Separately, **the same plant's rRNA fraction went 38.0% to 67.1% in eight months** and its identifiable fraction 22.7% to 40.7%, so a removal figure describes one sample on one day rather than wastewater. | first pass, 1 seed of 5; the instrument reproduces this repo's published 21.7% Kraken2 figure as 22.65% |
| [`dna_rna_pair/cross_witness_s1.json`](dna_rna_pair/REPORT.md) | Whether either library explains the other's unidentified reads, by exact 31-mer presence over whole libraries. **The DNA virome does not hold the RNA residue** — 13.3% of an unidentified read's 31-mers occur in it and 60.7% of those reads share nothing, with the gap running the wrong way, so a second extraction from the same water is not a cheap subtrahend. **But 71.2% of unidentified reads are witnessed in the same plant eight months earlier**, 14.1% sharing nothing: recurrence measured rather than inferred, and a warning that "absent from everything" mostly means absent from the databases. | live, 1 seed; **superseded as a recurrence figure** by `recurrence/`, which splits the ribosomal sequence out and adds the depth axis this one lacks |
| [`recurrence/`](recurrence/REPORT.md) | Cross-sample recurrence as the instrument the sampling wall cannot answer: depth 1 is depth 1 *in this run*, so the other libraries of PRJNA948850 turn one observation into an occupancy pattern. The panel is pinned by script — **116 paired libraries over 85 waters and 41 dates**, with the 99 `xh_*` runs excluded as the same RNA after xHYB hybrid capture — and the query set is stratified by this library's own depth rather than matched on it, so recurrence is a curve against the variable that confounds it. **All 115 other libraries, 84 waters, 40 dates, 502 days, five seeds in one scan, $1.78: at matched within-run depth the unplaced residue is absent from the entire plant 24 times as often as identified reads (21.5% against 0.9% at depth 0, ±0.6 points over five seeds), and the gap is nearly gone by depth 10–99 (1.4% against 0.0%), so depth 1 is two populations.** **Absence is missing molecules, not misread ones**: both mates unwitnessed over exactly one is 2.03 for the unplaced residue, 0.29 for identified, 0.00 for rRNA where it must be error, and ~1,000 on shuffles, so both ends of the field are pinned. **Transience is ruled out for whatever recurs at all**: at matched date-occupancy the residue's span in time is indistinguishable from known material (191 against 223 days on 2–3 dates, 3.8% against 4.1% inside a fortnight), with no burst signature anywhere. **And occupancy groups reads with no sequence comparison**: among pairs whose mates share no 31-mer, a residue read finds its own mate in its top 10 of 4,000 candidates 30–34% of the time against 0.5% chance and a 1.2–2.2% marginal-preserving curveball null — but the kNN excess over that null is only ~0.04 and the same size as in named material, so it is a density, never a clustering. Controls: own library 1.0000, every shuffle 0.0000 in 0 of 115, rRNA on all 40 dates for 97.8% of deep reads, subject-depth ρ = −0.02, and identified reads of the same within-run rarity on a median 37 of 40 dates, which is the detection-limit control the figure stands on. | live, 5 seeds; the remaining gap is that absence from 115 libraries cannot be told from permanently sub-single-copy material by this instrument |

| [`nontemplate_prefix/`](nontemplate_prefix/REPORT.md) | The 14 nt mate-2 trim, tested against SILVA on **untrimmed** reads with the other end of the same read as the control. **Mode exactly 14 in 5/5 seeds on both RNA libraries**, 95% of anchored R2 reads clipping ≥10 while 94.6% of the same reads sit flush at their 3' end; mate 1's mode is 0. The composition detector that had been setting the trim read 9 and 16 — not the prefix length. **But 16 is nonetheless the better trim, and "over-trim" was withdrawn the same day:** re-running at 14 made the Kraken2 fraction go *down* 1.7 points, because the prefix length is a distribution (mode 14 at 51%, with a tail) and Kraken2's confidence is a *fraction* of a read's minimizers. So the trim is an operating point, measured as a curve in `trim_sensitivity_*.json`, not a constant. Mate 1 has a 5' excess of ~2 bases but **no mode**, which is what separates priming bias from a non-template prefix. Incidentally a third rRNA instrument (35.0–35.5% / 61.8–62.2% at ≥60 bases) that reproduces the eight-month drift. | live, 5 seeds, one SHA and one script hash; the DNA library anchored 0–11 reads of 100,000 so it has no answer here |
| [`partial_match_subjects/`](partial_match_subjects/REPORT.md) | What the residue's partial core_nt matches actually match, with reads the cascade already identified as the control. **In the chance band (539 of 567) the two piles are identical to three figures** — median E 0.82/0.81, identity 87.8%/87.2%, 38/39 matched bases — so the shape describes the database, not the reads; the subjects there include a stick insect, a kiwi, chicken, mouse, rice and mackerel. **218 of 231 could not be aligned locally at all**, not even at k=9, against the reference BLAST named. The 28 significant ones are the opposite: ~74 of ~107 bases with identity **holding into the padding**, i.e. divergent relatives, so requirement 1.ii's shape has no population above significance and no meaning below it. | live, 1 run of 1,133 rows, not 5 seeds; mate 1 only; the aligned subset leans viral because the fetch cap correlates with organism |
| [`partial_eyeball/`](partial_eyeball/) | The **first attempt** at the same question, rendering reads against the reference segment they matched with the three fields that would test a record-boundary story (`to_record_end`, `molecule`, `q_stop`). It rendered **4 reads instead of 45**: the instance had regenerated its query set between launches, so the local FASTA shared 6 of 500 read names with the rows, and the selection silently fell through to whatever matched. `partial_alignment_eyeball.py` and `bigindex_queries.py` now refuse a set that names fewer than 90% of the rows' reads. | **superseded** by `partial_match_subjects/`, and kept because the failure is the lesson: a name-keyed join that silently drops non-matches produces a small, plausible, wrong file |
