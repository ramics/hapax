Generated by `experiments/report_reach.py` from `run2`. Seed 1. Floor for every sequence arm: E < 1e-05 over at least 60 aligned residues.


## 1. How far each method reaches, by pile

`queries with a hit` is out of the pile's size. `most distant` is the median over that pile of each contig's lowest-identity significant alignment; `furthest single` is the lowest of them. `nearest` is the median of each contig's *best* significant alignment.

**Read the two identity columns against each other and against the near-reference row.** The most-distant column is bounded below by the floor rather than by the query: at a fixed E-value the lowest-identity alignment a large database returns is set by the database's size and the query's length, so a near-reference contig and a divergent one come back at nearly the same number. That is the control moving with the test, and it means the most-distant statistic is a property of the operating point and not of the method. The nearest column is the one that separates piles, and `queries with a hit` is the one that separates arms.

It calibrates existence of an alignment, not any field conditional on one.

## 7. Codon space: is the synonymous signal saturated where these candidates sit

**Read the `nearest` block, not the `all` block.** `all` pools every significant tblastn alignment a pile produced, and a pile's alignments are dominated by its long tail of distant subjects rather than by its own nearest relative - so `all` reads the same on the divergent pile and on the 95-99% control, which is the subject set describing itself rather than the piles. `nearest` takes the best-scoring alignment per ORF, which is the relative the contig actually has. `dS undefined` is the Jukes-Cantor correction's argument going non-positive, which is what saturation looks like when it is not clipped to a finite number.
