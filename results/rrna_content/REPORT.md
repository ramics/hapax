# Ribosomal content of the Berlin library, and what it does to a protein index

Generated by `experiments/fill_rrna_report.py` from the JSON in this directory. Do
not edit the tables by hand.

## 1. Headline

1. **34.5% +/- 0.4% of SRR23973193 reads are ribosomal RNA**, by
   positive exact 31-mer containment against SILVA 138.1 SSU + LSU NR99. Five seeds,
   20,000 pairs each, the same read blocks as `experiments/absence_residual.py`.
2. The call is **95.3% sensitive** on ribosomal sequences held out of the
   index entirely, so the headline understates the true fraction by a few points at
   most *for taxa that have a close curated relative*. It says nothing about
   lineages with no near neighbour in SILVA, which are still missed. See §3.
3. **The abundant-non-coding explanation of the peptide-absence residue is dead.**
   Ribosomal reads make up 42.5% of absent-and-mapped reads but
   41.9% of present-and-mapped reads. No enrichment, so being ribosomal is
   not why a mapped read has no peptide match.
4. **The unmapped majority of the residue is not ribosomal either**: 3.6%.
   So "whole molecules missing from the sample's own assembly" survives as the
   reading of that bin, and the rare-and-unsequenced branch keeps the majority.
5. **The unexpected result: ribosomal reads are *less* likely to have every peptide
   10-mer absent than anything else**, 0.125 against 0.286. Ribosomal RNA has
   no true peptide, so an index of real proteins cannot explain this and chance
   cannot either (§5). Something in the index is matching them.
6. A gene caller handed nothing but curated ribosomal RNA calls genes on
   93.2% of sequences at a coding density of 0.164, which is real
   spurious protein entering any sample-self index — but a composition-shuffled
   control gives 0.179, *higher*, so this is generic short-ORF calling and
   not something ribosomal sequence specifically provokes. It is also too small to
   account for §5 on its own. **The mechanism is therefore established as a real
   contaminant of the index and NOT established as the explanation of §5.** §6.

## 2. The measurement

- Reference: SILVA 138.1 SSURef_NR99 + LSURef_NR99, 605,794 sequences,
  1,026,936,512 bases.
- Index: canonical 31-mers, subsampled 1 in
  8 by a splitmix64 hash of the k-mer's own 62-bit
  code, leaving 15,821,248 distinct codes. Membership is exact, so
  the structure contributes no false positives; subsampling by a hash of the code
  keeps the retained set a uniform sample rather than biasing it toward any taxon.
- That 15,821,248 is far below an eighth of
  1,026,936,512 positions because ribosomal RNA is conserved enough to be
  very redundant at this k. The index is 127 MB, which is why this fits at all.
- A read is called ribosomal at >= 3 retained hits out of the ~9.6 a 107 nt read
  contributes at this sampling rate.

The sampler is checked rather than trusted (`experiments/rrna_sampler_check.py`),
because the other branch hit a real bug of exactly this kind: masking the low bits of
an FNV-style hash over a four-letter alphabet retained 1 k-mer in 65 rather than 1 in
8, which is invisible in a headline because the same sampling applies to reference and
query alike. Masking the low bits of a splitmix64 finalizer instead gives a realised
rate of 0.12536 on reference sequence and
0.12486 on reads against a target of
0.12500, and 9.38 retained
k-mers per read against 9.50 expected.

## 3. Sensitivity and specificity, measured not assumed

`experiments/rrna_control.py`. Every 50th SILVA sequence is held out of the index
and reads are sampled from the held-out ones (12,116
sequences, 593,678 left in), at Berlin's measured
substitution rate of 0.001. Negatives are
107 nt reads from 6 non-ribosomal genomes
(`data/peptide_noise/`, two of them bacteriophage, so no host sequence either).

| min hits | sensitivity, held-out rRNA | false flag rate, non-rRNA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| >= 1 | 0.9812 | 0.00145 |
| >= 2 | 0.9697 | 0.00120 |
| >= 3 | 0.9530 | 0.00100 |
| >= 5 | 0.8837 | 0.00070 |
| >= 8 | 0.6303 | 0.00025 |

At the >= 3 rule used throughout, sensitivity is 0.9530 and the false flag rate
0.00100, so 34.5% corrects to
36.2% and the contamination from non-ribosomal sequence is around a
tenth of a percent against a signal of a third of the library.

**The limit of this control, stated because it bounds the headline's meaning.**
NR99 is clustered at 99% identity, so a held-out sequence usually has a relative
about a percent away still in the index. The sensitivity above is therefore the
sensitivity for organisms that *are* represented in SILVA, and a genuinely divergent
environmental lineage would still be missed by exact 31-mers. The headline remains a
lower bound; what this control establishes is that the slack is not in the
well-represented part, which is where a third of the library evidently sits.

Three things follow, and two of them contradict predictions both threads wrote down
in advance.

**The mapped part of the residue is not explained by being non-coding.** Ribosomal
reads are 42.5% of absent-and-mapped reads and 41.9% of
present-and-mapped reads. A non-coding explanation of the residue requires
enrichment and there is none. The branch I proposed in
`results/peptide_absence_noise/REPORT.md` §11 as a replacement for the withdrawn
"absence means wastewater" claim does not survive its own test.

**The unmapped part is not ribosomal**, 3.6%. The other branch's reading
of that bin as whole molecules missing from the sample's own assembly therefore
stands, and it is the majority of the residue. Both threads predicted 20-30%
ribosomal there and both were wrong by most of an order of magnitude.

**The ribosomal call behaves like ribosomal RNA should**, which is worth saying as a
check on the method rather than a finding: where these reads map, the contigs carry
median coverage 427 against 52 for non-ribosomal reads. The most
abundant thing in a total-RNA library is what it is picking up.

## 5. The result that needed explaining

P(absent | ribosomal) = 0.125 against 0.286 for everything else. Ribosomal RNA
encodes no protein, so no real entry in the index can match a ribosomal read, and
chance cannot do it either: a peptide 10-mer is drawn from a space of about 1e13, the
index holds about 2.2e8 of them, and a read offers at most 156 10-mers across six
frames, which puts the chance of any match near 0.3%. The observed 87.5% of
ribosomal reads having a match is three orders of magnitude away from that. Whatever
is matching them is in the index for a reason.

The non-absent ribosomal reads are 99.0% mapped, and 0.416 of them
fall inside a predicted gene against 0.131 for the absent ones. The index
includes proteins predicted by pyrodigal from the sample's own assembly
(`experiments/absence_residual.py` indexes viral proteins, Swiss-Prot and
`berlin_sample_proteins.faa`), so gene calls on ribosomal contigs are the obvious
candidate. §6 tests that, and only partly supports it.

## 6. What a gene caller actually does to ribosomal RNA

`experiments/prodigal_on_rrna.py`. pyrodigal in meta mode, the mode a metagenomic
assembly is annotated with, over 3,000 SILVA
sequences per seed (5.09 Mbp), five seeds. Each sequence is then shuffled,
preserving length and base composition while destroying every motif, as a control on
whether ribosomal sequence is specifically prone to this.

| | any gene called | coding density | mean gene length (aa) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| real rRNA | 0.9324 (0.0025) | 0.1636 (0.0016) | 71 |
| composition-shuffled | 0.9116 (0.0034) | 0.1786 (0.0023) | 76 |

So a gene caller does invent protein on ribosomal RNA, and at scale: 277,800
amino acids from 5.09 Mbp, on 93% of sequences. Every
sample-self protein index built by annotating an assembly and indexing the result
carries this, and ribosomal contigs are among the highest-coverage contigs in a
total-RNA library, so there are plenty of them.

Two honest limits on that, both of which cut against the strong form of the claim.

First, the shuffled control calls *more* (0.179 against 0.164), so this
is generic short-ORF calling on sequence of this composition and not something
ribosomal structure provokes. Mean called length is 71 aa, which is short.
The phenomenon is a property of running a gene caller on anything, and ribosomal RNA
is simply the most abundant anything in this library.

Second, 0.164 coding density is well below the 0.92
the other branch measured across the whole Berlin assembly, and below the
0.416 of non-absent ribosomal reads that fall inside a call. A density of
0.164 cannot by itself put 87% of ribosomal reads inside reach
of a spurious peptide. Either the spurious peptides are matched by reads outside
their own contig's calls, which ribosomal conservation makes easy, or part of §5 has
another cause, the obvious candidate being that translated ribosomal RNA is
low-complexity and matches the low-complexity regions of real proteins.

**So §6 establishes that this contaminates the index and does not establish that it
explains §5.** The decisive test is to rebuild the index with proteins from
ribosomal contigs excluded and re-measure P(absent | ribosomal): if the mechanism is
the explanation it should rise toward 0.286, and if it is not it will stay near
0.125. That test needs the assembly and the annotation, so it belongs to the
branch that holds them, and it is running there.

## 7. What this costs the thesis

The deliverable in view is a curve of background reads removed against index size,
and the cheapest large step on it so far was adding the sample's own predicted
proteins, which took absence from 86.3% to around 22%. Part of that step is a gene
caller explaining ribosomal reads with protein that does not exist. Until §6's
decisive test lands, the size of that part is unknown, and the step cannot be quoted
as background removed without saying so. The fix is cheap and should be stated as
method rather than caveat: mask ribosomal sequence before annotating, or count the
reads that only a self-predicted protein explains as their own category.

The other half of the ledger is simply useful. A third of this library is ribosomal
and a protein index can never match it, so a third of the sequencing is being spent
on material that the whole approach is structurally blind to. That is an argument
about library preparation, not about indexing, and it is the first number in this
project that bears on it.

## 8. What this does not establish

- Sensitivity is measured against a 99%-clustered reference, so it speaks for
  represented taxa only (§3). A divergent environmental lineage is still missed and
  the headline stays a lower bound.
- Ribosomal status is called per read. A read on a contig that is partly ribosomal
  and partly not is called ribosomal, which is one reason the in_cds figures here and
  a contig-level labelling need not agree.
- 0.51% of reads carry fewer than 3 retained
  k-mers, from ambiguous bases or length, so they can never be called ribosomal at
  any threshold. That is a ceiling on sensitivity rather than a tuning choice, and it
  is small enough not to matter at this effect size.
- One library, one sample. Berlin is a total-RNA preparation; a poly-A or
  ribodepleted library would not look like this, and the third is a property of this
  protocol rather than of wastewater.
- §6 runs one gene caller in one mode. It says what pyrodigal meta does, not what
  every annotation pipeline does.
- Nothing here touches sensitivity or the false-positive rate of a novelty call. It
  explains the composition of a background, which is a different question from
  whether a read can be scored.
