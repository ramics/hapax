# Subtract the known, then characterise what is left

Berlin wastewater SRR23973193, 5 seeds x 100,000 read pairs, measured 2026-11-20.
Scripts: `experiments/subtract_known.py`, `experiments/residue_report.py`,
`experiments/subsample_uniref.py`, aggregated by `experiments/report_subtract.py`.
Prior art and what is already occupied: `results/subtract_known/PRIOR_ART.md`.

The thesis under test is Imo's, in his words on 2026-11-20: *find the absolute most
efficient way to extract the known, then score the unknown*. "Efficient" is measured in
both senses at once - bytes of index and seconds of wall time on one axis, fraction of the
library actually removed on the other.

## 1. Why subtraction and not scoring

Every single-read feature this project has tried as a novelty score has failed for one
reason: a wastewater background is coding sequence too, so "looks like a gene" cannot
separate novel-coding from known-coding. That verdict covers ORF-likeness, k-mer
composition, codon usage, GC and codon-pair bias (PR #8,
`results/readlevel_features/REPORT.md`), and translated search, where score ordering
tracks database proximity rather than novelty.

Subtraction asks the index a question an index can answer - *is this already known* - so
the polarity problem does not arise. It moves the failure somewhere else, and that is what
this experiment is built to expose: **an incomplete index makes ordinary sewage look
novel**. PR #11 measured 86% of Berlin reads with no known peptide 10-mer against a viral
plus Swiss-Prot index, and the result below says most of that was the index, not the
sewage. So no absence number here appears without the index that produced it, priced in
bytes.

## 2. The cascade

Tiers are run independently over the same reads, which supports three readings: what each
index removes alone, what they remove as a union, and what the next tier adds in a stated
order. Only the protein tier runs on the survivors of the nucleotide tiers, because
DIAMOND costs more than the rest of the cascade together; that conditioning is recorded in
the output.

Every tier's threshold is swept rather than fixed. This is not decoration. On a mock
community Kraken2's classified fraction falls from ~51% at confidence 0.05 to ~5% at the
strictest setting, so a single classified-fraction number is a threshold choice wearing a
measurement's clothes. The operating point used in the cascade is stated with every table.

## 3. Results

All percentages are of READ PAIRS, which is the occurrence-weighted unit, not an average
over distinct k-mers. The distinction matters by orders of magnitude for conserved
sequence - on curated SILVA the mean number of entries reached per distinct peptide 10-mer
is 3.49 but 388 when weighted by occurrence - and a read sees the occurrence-weighted
quantity. Nothing in this report averages over distinct k-mers.

### 3.1 The cascade, 5 seeds x 100,000 pairs

Subtrahend defined by IDENTIFICATION, not by assembly: a contig that assembles but cannot
be identified stays in play, because removing it would swallow tier 2 whole. Operating
points: SILVA and contig tiers at >=40 matching bases, Kraken2 at confidence 0.1, DIAMOND
at bitscore >=50.

Three readings of that table.

**The sample's own assembly is the most efficient index by orders of magnitude.** 1.4 MB
of ribosomal contigs assembled from this very sample removes 38.85% alone, slightly more
than all 272 MB of SILVA; 15 MB of its identified non-rRNA contigs adds 30.33 points
marginally, where 16 GB of Kraken2 adds 0.42. Known-to-a-database and known-here are
different questions, and the cheap one is the second.

**Thresholds dominate database size.** PlusPF-16 removes 42.82% of the library at
confidence 0, 21.67% at 0.1 and 3.52% at 0.2 - a factor of twelve inside one database,
against a factor of 1.01 between 8 GB and 16 GB at a fixed threshold. Any published
"% unclassified" without a stated threshold is unreadable, which includes some of the
numbers this project has quoted at itself.

**The 86% unknown of PR #11 was mostly rRNA and index incompleteness.** rRNA alone is
38.02% of pairs at >=40 matching bases, and no protein index can ever match it. The
measurement agrees within about two points with the protein thread's 34.5% (36.2%
sensitivity-corrected) by exact 31-mer sharing, which is a different criterion; the
matching-base sweep gives 38.0 / 37.3 / 35.7 / 26.4 at 40 / 60 / 80 / 100 bases.

### 3.2 Ordering is a design requirement, not an accident

rRNA must be removed with a NUCLEOTIDE index before any protein index is consulted.
Against a large protein index ribosomal reads stop looking novel faster than anything
else, and the explanation that survives is annotation artefacts: public entries annotated
automatically that are really translated ribosomal sequence. The general mechanism is the
conservation multiplier measured database-independently by the now-closed protein thread;
the wastewater-specific confirmation is the wide-insert thread's entry counts, 63 of 4.1
million entries each reaching 1% or more of ribosomal reads and the top 100 carrying 52.2%
of all ribosomal read-matches, which is also a practical 63-entry blocklist. Without that
ordering a third of this library's score measures public annotation quality rather than
what is in the water. Enforced in `subtract_known.py` by tier order and asserted here
because it is a trap for anyone reimplementing the cascade.

### 3.3 Contig-level identification reverses the read-level picture

`experiments/classify_contigs.py` over this sample's 82,947 contigs (29.8 Mb), at Kraken2
confidence 0.1 or DIAMOND bitscore >=50 against Swiss-Prot and 10% of UniRef50:

- 34.78% of contigs identified, 49.13% of bases.
- Route: protein-only 26.30%, nucleotide-only 5.73%. At read length the nucleotide index
  does the work; at contig length the protein index does, by a factor of 4.6. That is the
  measured reason the cascade needs both and the reason tier 2 must be defined by
  identification.
- rRNA is only 4.38% of contigs and 3.94% of bases, at median coverage 44.7x against 1.27x
  overall. So the sample's-own-assembly step is not rRNA leakage wearing a different hat;
  the ribosomal contigs are reported as their own tier above and remove 38.85% on their
  own.

**Tier 2 is real but thin.** 46,157 contigs cannot be identified at all, 14.3 Mb, median
length 254 nt and median coverage 1.12x. Only 3.87% of them - about 1,784 contigs - clear
both 500 nt and 2x, which is where PR #5 measured that assembly and classification begin
to work at all. Tier 2 is therefore a small, honest pile rather than the easy win the
three-tier sketch implies, and its 1,784 members are the right size for the per-contig
characterisation Imo asked for.

### 3.4 Ground truth: an actual BLAST against NCBI core_nt

`experiments/remote_blast_check.py` draws a stratified sample, 60 mate-1 reads per class,
labels every class locally with the cascade's own references and thresholds, and submits
them to NCBI's remote BLAST (blastn against nt, 133 M sequences / 1.1 Tbase - two orders
of magnitude more sequence than any index that fits here). Seed 1:

| class | % of library | any hit | median identity | median query coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rRNA | 37.6 | 60/60 | 100.0 | 0.99 |
| public nucleotide (Kraken2) | 2.7 | 60/60 | 100.0 | 0.99 |
| identified by the sample's own assembly | 34.3 | 53/60 | 91.6 | 0.64 |
| residue, maps an unidentified contig | 9.1 | 49/60 | 90.6 | 0.34 |
| residue, maps nothing | 16.3 | 52/60 | 88.9 | 0.38 |
| tier-2 contigs (>=500 nt, >=2x) | - | 58/60 | 86.7 | 0.07 |

The headline is a correction to our own language. **The residue is not matchless.** Against
1.1 Tbase, 87% of residue reads hit something - so "matches nothing" was always shorthand
for "matches nothing in an index that fits on this disk". What separates the classes is not
whether they hit but how much of the read is covered at what identity: a ribosomal read
aligns over its whole length at 100%, a residue read aligns over a third of itself at 89%.
That is precisely the distant-partial-homology signature, and it is why the per-read report
reports closest relative as a continuous field and never thresholds it.

It is also independent support for the coordinator's point that the index-size curve has
not flattened: the sequence our indexes miss is findable in a database 70 times larger
than anything local, so removal is still rising with index size at the largest index
reachable. Five seeds and a blastx pass against nr on the residue and tier-2 classes are
running; this table is seed 1 only and is labelled as such.

### 3.5 Does the removal curve flatten? Not on this evidence

The cascade's three Kraken2 sizes (0.67, 8, 16 GB) gain 0.42 points on the last doubling,
which reads like exhaustion. The protein thread made exactly that mistake in the other
direction - two small virus-weighted points read as "protein databases barely help",
overturned when a tenth of UniRef50 moved the same quantity 23 points - so three points on
a line are not a curve here either.

`experiments/nucleotide_ladder.py` puts more points on the nucleotide axis without
building databases this environment cannot build: classify once against the largest
database available, then ask what removal would have been had only a nested random subset
of REFERENCE TAXA been present. The axis is reference content, not bytes. Both biases run
the same way - deleting calls loses the reads a smaller real database would still place on
a retained relative, and Kraken2's capped hash means bytes grow sublinearly with taxa - so
each point understates, which makes this a conservative test.

| reference content | taxa | removed, conf 0.0 | marginal | removed, conf 0.1 | marginal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3% | 6 | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| 1% | 20 | 0.24 | 0.20 | 0.05 | 0.04 |
| 3% | 57 | 1.00 | 0.75 | 0.15 | 0.11 |
| 10% | 202 | 3.68 | 2.68 | 1.49 | 1.34 |
| 30% | 642 | 9.42 | 5.75 | 2.60 | 1.11 |
| 50% | 1,086 | 16.88 | 7.46 | 6.37 | 3.77 |
| 100% | 2,155 | 42.82 | 25.94 | 21.67 | 15.30 |

No plateau appears: the last doubling of content still buys 15-26 points. **But the
instrument is weak and the reason is worth stating.** Reads are heavily concentrated over
reference taxa - at confidence 0.1 the single top taxon carries 49.0% of classified reads,
the top ten 86.4% - so most of that last step is one dominant taxon being inside the
subset rather than outside it. This ladder supports "has not visibly saturated" and does
not resolve the curve's shape; the per-seed spread (min-max in
`results/nucleotide_ladder/TABLES.md`) is wide for the same reason and is reported rather
than averaged away.

The stronger nucleotide evidence is section 3.4's BLAST: against a database two orders of
magnitude larger than anything local, 87% of residue reads find a partial, distant match.
Sequence our indexes miss is findable in a database 70 times larger, which is what "the
curve has not flattened" means operationally. On the protein axis the same question is
measured directly on this cascade's residue in `results/residue_protladder/`.

### 3.6 The positive control: how divergent must a virus be to survive the cascade?

`experiments/spike_holdout.py`. Three real genomes - pepper mild mottle virus
(NC_003630.1), human astrovirus (NC_001943.1) and human rhinovirus 1 (NC_038311.1) - are
simulated into this library's own measured insert distribution (median 194, p95 363) and
its own measured error rates (0.00089 on R1, 0.00117 on R2), then walked away from
themselves by uniform substitution and pushed through every index the cascade subtracts.
2,000 pairs per genome per rung, 5 seeds.

A spike removed at tier 1 by a public database is a false negative of the whole
architecture, so this is the number that matters most.

| divergence | spike pairs surviving tier 1 |
|---|---|
| 0% | 0.0% (0.0-0.0 across seeds) |
| 5% | 0.6% (0.0-2.0) |
| 10% | 12.7% (6.4-19.2) |
| 20% | 82.2% (73.8-89.8) |
| 30% | 99.5% (98.2-100.0) |

The 0% rung is the control that the cascade works at all: a deposited virus spiked into
the library is removed completely. Recognition then falls away between 10% and 20%
nucleotide divergence, which is this pipeline's measured boundary between *already known*
and *new*.

**Which tier does the recognising** (mean % of spike pairs removed by each index alone):

| divergence | sample's contigs | Kraken2 viral | Kraken2 PlusPF-16 | viral proteins | Swiss-Prot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 65.5 | 100.0 | 64.0 | 97.5 | 97.5 |
| 5% | 51.6 | 86.6 | 5.9 | 94.6 | 94.7 |
| 10% | 21.8 | 28.1 | 0.4 | 82.9 | 83.0 |
| 20% | 0.2 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 17.4 | 17.6 |
| 30% | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.5 | 0.5 |

**The nucleotide side dies first and the protein index is the last thing standing.** By
10% divergence nucleotide identification has fallen to a quarter while translated search
still removes 84%; by 20% nucleotide is at zero and only the protein index holds anything
at all. That is the same asymmetry section 3.3 found at contig level, now measured as a
function of distance rather than of length, and it is the argument for keeping a protein
tier in a cascade whose cheapest and most effective tiers are nucleotide.

**Two of the three genomes turned out to be in this sample already.** Pepper mild mottle
virus assembles here at 285x coverage and human astrovirus as a 6,823 nt contig at 11.7x;
human rhinovirus 1 does not appear in this sample's assembly at all. That was discovered
while checking an abundance reading that looked like a sketch artefact and was not: a
PMMoV read recurs in the library because PMMoV is genuinely abundant in sewage. It is
reported here rather than quietly fixed because it changes how the 0% rung reads - part of
that removal is the sample recognising itself - and because rhinovirus is therefore the
clean holdout of the three. The survival ladder is unaffected in shape: the per-tier table
above separates the sample's own contigs from the public indexes, and the two columns fall
away together.

**Limits of this control.** Uniform substitution is a crude model of real divergence -
selection shapes it, codon structure constrains it, synonymous sites carry most of it - so
the percentages are ordinal, not family distances. No indels are applied, deliberately: a
frameshift would collapse the coding fields for reasons unrelated to how far away a
relative is.

**At 1 in a million.** A virus at that abundance contributes about 8.7 read pairs in this
8,731,224-pair run. Per-read survival above says roughly one of them reaches the per-read
report at 10% divergence (8.7 x 0.127 = 1.1 pairs) and seven at 20% (8.7 x 0.822 = 7.2). Per-read behaviour is measured at a spike rate
high enough to resolve it and the 1-in-a-million statement is arithmetic on top; nothing
here was measured at that rate directly, because a sample that holds 8.7 pairs cannot
resolve a rate. That was the error of PR #11, where 1-per-million thresholds came from
zero counts in a 20,000-read run, and it is not repeated here. The one field whose value
really does depend on abundance - library recurrence - is measured with exactly the number
of spike pairs 1-in-a-million implies:

| | median 31-mer count in 400,000 pairs |
|---|---|
| pepper mild mottle virus (abundant here) | 10, 18, 23, 18, 7 across seeds |
| human astrovirus | 1 in every seed |
| human rhinovirus 1 (the clean holdout) | 1 in every seed |
| a typical background read | 67.6 |

So a virus at 1 in a million reads as a singleton against a background whose typical read
recurs about 68 times, and the field does separate the two. PMMoV does not, because it is
genuinely abundant in sewage - which is the field working, not failing. The count-min
sketch only overstates (saturation 0.006%), so a read it calls rare is rare for certain.
The sketch block is the same 400,000 pairs in every seed by design, since it stands for
"the library", so the seeds vary the spike and not the background: the background number
is one measurement, not five.

### 3.7 "Half a read matches high, the other half is gibberish" - what is actually going on

Requirement 1.ii, measured by `experiments/partial_alignment_anatomy.py`, which takes
partially-matching sequence and asks which of four things it is, with the reads the cascade
*identified* as a control population throughout. The four:

- **A, chimaera**: the unmatched piece aligns to a *different* molecule assembled in this
  sample. That is the hypothesis Imo named, and it is the only one of the four that is a
  statement about the library rather than about the database.
- **B, divergent continuation**: the unmatched piece returns to the *same* subject, more
  weakly. One molecule, half of it past the search's recognition floor.
- **C, artefact end**: the unmatched piece is low-entropy, homopolymeric or adapter.
- **D, short true homology**: nothing fires - the match is simply short.

**At read level this question is unanswerable, and that is the result.** At 107 nt, when a
read matches over ~74% of itself the unmatched remainder is about 10 aa, below any protein
search's floor, so the discriminating test returns nothing for 99.6% of reads. The split-
mapping test fires on 13.4% of partial residue reads but 37.4% of already-identified ones:
it measures mappability, not chimaerism. Partial alignment is not even enriched in the
residue (23.8% vs 19.7%) - it is a property of short reads meeting a protein database. What
separates the two piles is identity, continuously: 51.9% vs 63.2%.

**At contig level it is answerable, and the answer is a negative on chimaerism.** 800
unidentifiable and 800 identified contigs of >=500 nt, DIAMOND blastp against Swiss-Prot,
split segments re-queried:

| | unidentifiable | identified (control) |
|---|---|---|
| any protein hit | 601/800 | 800/800 |
| mean aligned fraction of the contig | 0.327 | 0.593 |
| mean identity | 35.1% | 39.5% |
| partial hits (15-75% aligned) | 85.4% of hits | 68.4% |
| A chimaera | **0.0%** | **0.0%** |
| B divergent continuation | 0.6% | 3.7% |
| C artefact end | 0.8% | 1.3% |
| D short true homology | 98.6% | 95.1% |

So: **no evidence that the unmatched part of a partially-matching contig comes from another
molecule present in this sample.** Zero in both piles, including the control, where a
mis-assembly would be just as visible. The limit is explicit: this test can only see a
chimaera whose partner also assembled here, so it cannot exclude a join to something too
rare to assemble.

B is the instructive one. Divergent continuation fires six times *more often* on already-
identified contigs than on unidentifiable ones, so it flags familiarity, not novelty: you
only observe a weak return to the same subject when the subject is well represented to
begin with. A pipeline that read B as "divergent, therefore interesting" would be reading
database coverage backwards.

What does separate the piles is simply how much matches at all - a continuous quantity,
0.327 against 0.593 aligned fraction - and not any categorical verdict about the unmatched
remainder. This is also the first field measured here that works on contigs and fails on
reads, which is an argument about where in a production tool this work belongs.

Single seed so far; the 5-seed run is queued. A_chimaera at exactly zero in 1,600 contigs
needs the other seeds before it is quoted as a rate rather than as "not detectable here".

## 4. What the residue is, and is not

The 22.90% residue breaks down as follows, measured by mapping it back to assemblies
rather than to public databases:

- **8.42% abundant-but-unplaceable** (8.33-8.51 across seeds): assembles here, cannot be
  identified against anything. This is tier 2's read-level population and the pile where
  longer contigs and better classifiers would help.
- **9.97% maps a contig from one of the other 115 samples** of the same study
  (9.86-10.04). Observed, deliberately NOT subtracted: subtracting it would make
  cross-sample recurrence zero by construction and destroy a field the per-read report
  needs. The two categories above overlap, so they do not sum.
- **10.90% genuinely missing** (10.76-11.07): nothing in public databases, nothing in this
  sample's own assembly, nothing in the other 115 samples' assemblies. Rare AND
  unsequenced, which is also the description of a novel virus at 1 in a million - so our
  target sits on the same side of this feature as the irreducible background, and that is
  a limit of the architecture rather than of the implementation.

Subtracting the sample's OWN data is what turns the residue from "merely undeposited" into
"genuinely rare". That is the narrower replacement for the claim withdrawn in PR #13
("absence in wastewater means wastewater"), which failed because the organisms were in the
sample's own contigs, just not in public databases.

The residue is also, by construction, enriched for artefacts: a pile defined by matching
nothing collects adapters, primer dimers, chimaeras and low-complexity sequence. The
cheap artefact tier removes 0.31% of the library before any database is consulted, and
every residue row carries its own artefact fields plus the mean error probability from its
quality string, so an unexplainable read can be read as "probably instrument" rather than
"probably biology".

## 6. Calibration and limits

Stated up front because every number here is one-sided.

- **Removal is a lower bound on "known".** An index cannot distinguish sequence with no
  relative anywhere from sequence whose relative is below the matching threshold. The
  sweeps are the honest form of that limit.
- **Subtraction is not free.** The SURPI+ evaluation measured pre-processing removing
  0.43% of true viral reads. Any read this cascade removes could in principle be a
  divergent member of a known family, and at the 1-in-a-million target even a small
  false-removal rate matters more than the headline removal does.
- **The far end of the index axis is extrapolated, not measured.** A DIAMOND database of
  full UniRef50 is ~30 GB against ~15 GB of writable disk left after the nucleotide
  indexes, so UniRef50 enters as streamed nested 2% and 10% subsets. The shape of removal
  against index size is measured; the value at 100% is an extrapolation and is labelled as
  one. A run against the far end needs disk this
  container does not have; the streaming recipe in `experiments/subsample_uniref.py`
  already does the hard part. Hardware for it has been offered (us-east-1, spot, up to
  2 TB of EBS, 8 vCPU and about 64 GB of RAM), so the far end is now a decision rather
  than a wall - see the next point.
- **Which database the far end should be, and why it is not nr.** Scaling from the built
  10% subset (1.82 GB of DIAMOND database for 4.12 M records), UniRef50 is ~18 GB,
  UniRef90 ~56 GB and UniRef100 ~100 GB; a DIAMOND nr database is a few hundred. With the
  disk ceiling at 2 TB all four fit, so nr is affordable and the question is whether it is
  worth it. **The plan is UniRef100, not nr, and this is a claim about content rather than
  about cost:** TrEMBL is translated GenBank/EMBL/DDBJ CDS, so nr's protein content is
  largely UniProt's, clustered differently; what nr adds over UniRef100 is redundancy,
  PDB and RefSeq model proteins. That is an argument, not a measurement, so it gets a
  measurement: the remote NCBI translated pass runs the same sampled reads against the
  real nr, and if its identity distribution matches UniRef100's on those reads, nr buys
  nothing here and the cheaper index is the right one. If the distributions differ, nr
  goes on the disk that is now available. Either way the report will say which, and the
  binding constraint is no longer disk but RAM at ~64 GB and search time, which section
  3.8's scaling measurement prices.
- **The curve has not flattened.** The protein side's absence-versus-index-size curve was
  still buying about 20 points per tenfold step at the largest index reachable (PR #10
  sections 4.6-4.7), so "removal per gigabyte and where it flattens" currently has no
  flattening point and this report does not invent one. The nucleotide analogue is
  measured in section 3.5 rather than assumed from three Kraken2 points.
- **The all-sample assembly tier is a lower bound**: the GEO file truncates mid-stream at
  sample S65 of 116 (recorded in `data/MANIFEST.md`), so that tier subtracts less than a
  complete file would.
- **Read-level abundance is relative to a sample of the run**, 400,000 pairs, not a genome
  coverage depth. A count-min sketch only ever overstates a count, so a read called rare
  is rare for certain - the error runs in the safe direction for that claim.
